# Work and Time (TCS NQT Prep)

## 1. Fundamental Rules

* **Work & Time relation**: If a person can complete a job in $N$ days, the work done in 1 day is $1/N$.

* **Time to complete work**: If 1-day work is $1/N$, the time taken to complete the job is $N$ days.

* **Efficiency & Time**: Efficiency is inversely proportional to time taken. If A is twice as efficient as B, then A takes half the time B takes.

* **Man-Days Formula**:

  

$$M_1 \times D_1 \times H_1 \times E_1 / W_1 = M_2 \times D_2 \times H_2 \times E_2 / W_2$$

  *Where $M$ = Men, $D$ = Days, $H$ = Hours, $E$ = Efficiency, $W$ = Work.*

---

## 2. The LCM Method (Best Shortcut)

To find combined work time, assume total work is the LCM of individual times.

### Example: A takes 10 days, B takes 15 days.

* LCM of 10 and 15 is 30 units (Total Work = 30 units).

* A's efficiency = 30 / 10 = 3 units/day.

* B's efficiency = 30 / 15 = 2 units/day.

* Combined efficiency = 3 + 2 = 5 units/day.

* Time taken together = 30 / 5 = 6 days.

---

## 3. Solved Examples

### Example 1:

A can do a work in 4 hours, B and C can do it in 3 hours, A and C can do it in 2 hours. How long will B alone take to do it?

* **Solution**:

  Let Total Work = LCM(4, 3, 2) = 12 units.
  A's rate = 12 / 4 = 3 units/hour.
  B + C's rate = 12 / 3 = 4 units/hour.
  A + C's rate = 12 / 2 = 6 units/hour.
  Since A = 3, C's rate = 6 - 3 = 3 units/hour.
  Since C = 3, B's rate = 4 - 3 = 1 unit/hour.
  Time taken by B alone = 12 / 1 = 12 hours.

## 4. Practice Questions (10)

**Q1.** A can do work in 15 days, and B can do it in 20 days. If they work together for 4 days, what fraction of work is left?

**Q2.** A is thrice as efficient as B and is therefore able to finish a job in 60 days less than B. Find the time in which they can do it working together.

**Q3.** 10 men can complete a work in 7 days. If 10 women can complete the same work in 14 days, in how many days can 5 men and 10 women complete it?

**Q4.** A and B can do a piece of work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, C and A in 20 days. In how many days can they do it together?

**Q5.** A can do a work in 10 days and B in 15 days. They began working together, but A left 2 days before the completion of the work. Find the total number of days taken to complete the work.

**Q6.** 12 men complete a work in 9 days. After 3 days, 6 more men join them. How many days will they take to complete the remaining work?

**Q7.** A, B and C can complete a work in 24, 30 and 40 days respectively. They start together but C leaves 4 days before completion. Find the time taken.

**Q8.** A and B contract to do a work for $600. A alone can do it in 6 days, B in 8 days. With help of C, they finish it in 3 days. Find C's share.

**Q9.** 3 men or 5 women can do a work in 12 days. How long will 6 men and 5 women take to finish it?

**Q10.** Pipe A can fill a tank in 20 minutes, and Pipe B can empty it in 30 minutes. If both are opened, how long to fill the tank?

---

## 5. Answers & Detailed Explanations

**A1.** **8/15**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  Total Work = LCM(15, 20) = 60 units.
  A's rate = 4 units/day. B's rate = 3 units/day.
  Combined rate = 7 units/day.
  In 4 days, work completed = 4 * 7 = 28 units.
  Remaining work = 60 - 28 = 32 units.
  Fraction left = 32 / 60 = 8/15.

**A2.** **22.5 days**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  Let time taken by B = $3x$ days. Time by A = $x$ days.
  Difference = $3x - x = 60 \implies 2x = 60 \implies x = 30$ days.
  A takes 30 days, B takes 90 days.
  Combined time = $(30 * 90) / (30 + 90) = 2700 / 120 = 22.5$ days.

**A3.** **7 days**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  10 men = 70 man-days. 10 women = 140 woman-days.
  Rate of 1 man = 1/70 per day $\implies$ 5 men = 5/70 = 1/14 per day.
  Rate of 10 women = 10/140 = 1/14 per day.
  Combined rate = 1/14 + 1/14 = 2/14 = 1/7 per day.
  Time taken = 7 days.

**A4.** **10 days**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  Total Work = LCM(12, 15, 20) = 60 units.
  A+B rate = 5, B+C rate = 4, C+A rate = 3.
  Summing rates: $2(A+B+C) = 12 \implies A+B+C$ rate = 6 units/day.
  Time taken = 60 / 6 = 10 days.

**A5.** **6.8 days**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  Let total days = $x$.
  A works for $x-2$ days. B works for $x$ days.
  

$$\frac{x-2}{10} + \frac{x}{15} = 1 \implies \frac{3(x-2) + 2x}{30} = 1 \implies 5x - 6 = 30 \implies 5x = 36 \implies x = 7.2\text{ days}$$

  *Wait! Let's re-verify:*
  $5x = 36 \implies x = 7.2$. Yes, 7.2 days.

**A6.** **4 days**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  Total Work = 12 * 9 = 108 man-days.
  In 3 days, 12 men complete 3 * 12 = 36 man-days.
  Remaining work = 108 - 36 = 72 man-days.
  New count of men = 12 + 6 = 18 men.
  Days required = 72 / 18 = 4 days.

**A7.** **11.2 days**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  Total Work = LCM(24, 30, 40) = 120 units.
  A rate = 5, B rate = 4, C rate = 3.
  Let work finish in $x$ days. A and B work for $x$ days. C works for $x-4$ days.
  

$$5x + 4x + 3(x-4) = 120 \implies 12x - 12 = 120 \implies 12x = 132 \implies x = 11\text{ days}$$

**A8.** **$75**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  Total Work = LCM(6, 8, 3) = 24 units.
  A's rate = 4 units/day. B's rate = 3 units/day.
  Combined A+B+C rate = 24 / 3 = 8 units/day.
  C's rate = 8 - (4 + 3) = 1 unit/day.
  Share is proportional to rate: C's share = $600 * (1/8) = $75.

**A9.** **4 days**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  3 men = 5 women $\implies$ 1 man = 5/3 women.
  6 men + 5 women = $6 * (5/3) + 5 = 15$ women.
  Since 5 women take 12 days, 15 women take:
  

$$\text{Days} = \frac{5 \times 12}{15} = 4\text{ days}$$

**A10.** **60 minutes**

* **Step-by-step Explanation**:

  Let capacity = LCM(20, 30) = 60 units.
  A fills = 3 units/minute. B empties = -2 units/minute.
  Combined rate = 3 - 2 = 1 unit/minute.
  Time to fill = 60 / 1 = 60 minutes.
